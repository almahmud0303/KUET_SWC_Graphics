KUET STUDENT WELFARE CENTER (SWC)
Architectural reference for the 3D graphics project
Based on the 30 photographs in the project folder

1. PURPOSE AND ACCURACY

This document explains the visible SWC building, its surroundings, and how to
represent them using the project's geometric primitives and graphics techniques.
The photographs show the main entrance, curved brick facade, cafeteria, side
elevations, courtyard, balconies, corridors, stage, and landscaping.

Visible features are described as observations. Dimensions are starting estimates
for modeling, not measured dimensions. The complete footprint, concealed rooms,
roof construction, and connections behind walls cannot be established exactly
from these photographs. No compass orientation is confirmed by the images.

2. OVERALL BUILDING ORGANIZATION

The SWC consists of connected rectangular wings surrounding a large grass
courtyard. Several wings have two visible occupied levels, while the taller
cafeteria/corridor block shows three levels in some views. Do not give every wing
the same height or number of floors.

The architectural character comes from pale concrete frames, exposed red brick,
flat projecting roof slabs, square columns, repetitive windows, and open covered
circulation. The front has a broad entrance portico and a prominent curved brick
volume on the viewer's right. A paved plaza lies in front of the entrance.

Inside, the lawn is bordered by raised walkways and corridors. Open upper-level
balconies look toward the lawn. A brick backdrop and low curved stage/platform
occupy one side of the courtyard. The exact courtyard enclosure should remain
adjustable until a measured plan or an overhead reference is available.

3. MAIN FRONT ENTRANCE

A broad, flat white canopy covers a double-height entrance space. Tall square
columns stand well in front of the recessed walls and doors. The roof's underside
is mostly plain, with small dark surface-mounted light fixtures.

Broad pale steps connect the red-paved plaza to the raised entrance floor. The
main timber doorway has multiple panels and a row of louvered panels above it.
Warm orange-brown wall panels flank the entrance. Openings above and behind the
door area reveal upper circulation and a deeper connection through the building.

The visual depth is important: place columns, balcony edges, walls, and doors on
separate depth planes. Build actual openings around the entrance and corridors.
Large banners hang from the canopy in several photos. These, along with event
boards and balloon decorations, are temporary details rather than building form.

4. CURVED BRICK SWC FACADE

The front-right feature is a tall convex red-brick wall carrying Bengali and
English lettering for the Student Welfare Center. It projects beyond the nearby
recessed corridor and extends above the adjacent canopy.

Its silhouette is curved in plan and has a visibly slanted/flared left edge.
Photographs do not establish an exact radius or prove that the curve is circular
or elliptical. Use an adjustable curved wall segment, then compare its outline
against the front and oblique photos.

Thin pale horizontal bands divide the brick surface. Tall narrow louver strips
with projecting pale vertical fins frame the lettering area. Brick courses wrap
around the curve, and the base is a darker brick plinth. Lawn and small shrubs
surround the front and side of this volume.

For implementation, generate a curved wall from connected vertical quads, with
inner and outer surfaces and edge caps. Start with 48-64 segments across the
visible arc. Add the pale bands as matching narrow curved strips. Keep radius,
arc angle, height, and taper adjustable. A full solid cylinder will need changes
to reproduce this partial, flared facade accurately.

5. WINDOWED WINGS

The long windowed wings contain a strong repeated grid. Large pale columns mark
structural bays, with smaller piers dividing windows within each bay. A continuous
horizontal beam separates floors. The projecting canopy shades the facade and
the walkway beneath it.

A typical window assembly contains:
- A recessed opening inside a pale concrete surround.
- A short horizontal transom above the main glazing.
- Two or more framed glass panels below the transom.
- Dense horizontal and vertical metal security bars.
- An exposed-brick panel beneath the glass.
- A slightly projecting brick sill or cap.

Glass varies from dark green-grey to pale blue-grey because of reflections and
interior darkness. It should not appear uniformly bright blue. Some panes are
open, showing dark gaps. Upper windows on the long two-level wing have external
AC units mounted on metal brackets near sill level.

6. BALCONIES, COURTYARD FRAMES, AND CORRIDORS

Upper courtyard corridors have pale solid parapets with long rectangular brick
insets. Thin horizontal metal rails run above the solid panels and across gaps.
Doors and windows sit behind these balcony edges in deep shade.

Some courtyard bays have projecting rectangular concrete frames: tall uprights
joined by horizontal beams. These create open rectangular spaces in front of the
upper corridor. They are distinct from the enclosed room walls behind them.
A short run of closely spaced overhead slats is visible near a wing junction.

The covered ground-floor corridor has repeated square columns along the lawn,
transverse ceiling beams, pale floor tiles, a dark edge strip, and scattered dark
square tile accents. Wooden doors and high rectangular openings line its inner
wall. Black cylindrical ceiling lights and long tube fixtures are visible.
A staircase can be seen at the far end of the corridor photo.

Keep the corridor floor slightly above the lawn. Use steps at photographed access
points. The shaded corridor should remain readable while being darker than the
sunlit grass and external columns.

7. COURTYARD AND BRICK STAGE

The courtyard is a broad open grass area with relatively sparse planting. Small
trees and flowering shrubs tend to stand near the building edges. It is visually
more open than the densely landscaped external paths.

One wing has a large rectangular red-brick backdrop in front of the building.
A raised platform and low curved brick edge extend into the courtyard. Short
pale steps are visible toward the right side in the closer oblique view.
The structure appears stage-like; its precise function is not established by
the photographs alone.

Model the backdrop with a thick box. Build the platform separately and construct
the curved edge from an extruded arc or short connected wall segments. Preserve
the relationship between this feature, the balcony behind it, and the open lawn.

8. CAFETERIA AND ADJACENT EXTERIOR

The cafeteria has a recessed ground-floor entrance beneath a projecting balcony
or canopy bearing Bengali lettering and the English word CAFETERIA. Windows
flank the dark doorway. The entrance is reached by broad shallow steps.

A straight external staircase rises immediately to the right. It has pale treads
and a brick side element. A rectangular planter stands near its lower end. Above
the cafeteria sign is a transparent-looking railing with metal posts. A tall
rectangular louver panel occupies the wall above. The adjacent building shows
multiple levels of windows and brick infill panels.

Nearby exterior views show shopfronts, shutters, signs, AC units, a gated opening,
brick paving, and planting. These belong to a more detailed secondary facade and
should be placed using the corresponding photos rather than repeated everywhere.

9. FRONT PLAZA AND PATHS

The front plaza is paved in red/terracotta rectangular units, interrupted by small
tan square groups. A large circular multicolored alpona/rangoli-style painting
lies in front of the entrance. It contains concentric borders, petal-like forms,
and repeated radial motifs in white, green, yellow, blue, and red.

Use a flat paved surface with small color variations. Place the circular artwork
slightly above it to avoid depth flickering. Annuli create the borders, while
radially repeated triangles or custom petal meshes add recognizable detail.

Outside the building, a concrete pedestrian/campus road runs beside clipped
hedges. Narrow red-paver paths connect to entrances. Small drainage channels or
grated strips occur beside some landscaped edges. Bicycles and motorcycles in
the entrance photos are optional movable props.

10. GAZEBO AND LANDSCAPING

A bamboo shelter stands near the path and cafeteria-side landscaping. It has
slender posts, low woven panels, a lattice band, open sides above the railing,
and a broad thatched hipped roof. The exact footprint and number of posts are
partly obscured, so use an adjustable polygonal or rectangular shelter.

The landscape includes coconut palms, fan-leaved palms, large branching trees,
small ornamental trees, clipped hedges, rounded shrubs, and red/yellow ornamental
foliage. Many trunks have a white-painted lower section topped by a narrow red
band. Vary tree height, crown shape, and spacing to match the views.

Primitive choices:
- Trunks and branches: tapered or connected cylinders.
- Rounded crowns: overlapping spheres with varied size and green shades.
- Palm fronds: bent strips or groups of narrow triangles.
- Hedges: repeated overlapping low foliage clusters.
- Gazebo posts: thin cylinders; wall panels: quads and crossed thin beams.
- Thatched roof: sloping triangular/quadrilateral faces with tan variations.

11. STARTING PROPORTIONS

These are modeling assumptions, not measurements extracted from the photos.
Choose one consistent unit scale and refine it against several viewpoints.

Component                         Suggested starting range
Floor-to-floor height             3.3-3.8 units
Two-level roof height             7.0-8.0 units above surrounding ground
Square column width               0.40-0.60 units
Structural bay spacing            2.7-3.8 units, depending on wing
Covered corridor width            2.4-3.2 units
Roof fascia/slab visible depth     0.30-0.55 units
Individual window width            1.4-1.9 units
Window sill height                0.65-0.90 units above floor
Balcony guard total height         0.95-1.15 units
Step riser                        0.12-0.17 units
Step tread                        0.28-0.35 units

Treat one unit as approximately one metre if convenient. Estimate overall wing
lengths from the chosen bay spacing and the visible bay counts. Do not assume
the roof thickness is equal to the larger beams visible below it. Perspective
and tree cover prevent reliable exact totals for all columns and windows.

12. MATERIALS AND LIGHTING

Suggested base RGB values in the range 0 to 1:
Pale concrete     (0.84, 0.87, 0.83)
Light trim        (0.94, 0.95, 0.91)
Red brick         (0.50, 0.20, 0.13)
Light brick       (0.62, 0.31, 0.22)
Dark glass        (0.10, 0.18, 0.18)
Metal frames      (0.72, 0.76, 0.73)
Wood doors        (0.29, 0.12, 0.06)
Terracotta paving (0.53, 0.27, 0.20)
Tan accents       (0.67, 0.47, 0.31)
Grass             (0.14, 0.43, 0.13)

These are starting colors, not calibrated samples. Let lighting create shaded
surfaces instead of assigning every shaded face a permanently dark base color.
Use low specular response for brick and concrete, higher response for metal and
glass, and moderate ambient light so covered passages remain visible.

Several photos show strong sunshine; later front views show softer illumination.
Choose one consistent sun direction for the scene. A Phong fragment shader gives
surface lighting, but it does not automatically cast shadows from roofs or trees.
Matching those shadows requires a separate shadow technique, such as shadow
mapping. Glass reflections also require additional treatment beyond plain Phong
highlights if photographic realism is desired.

13. CONNECTION TO THE GRAPHICS REQUIREMENTS

Use boxes for slabs, beams, columns, parapets, doors, AC housings, and stairs.
Use cylinders for poles, railings, tree trunks, and light fixtures. Use spheres
for foliage and small rounded props. Quads and triangles support curved walls,
roof faces, glazing, and palm leaves. Annuli support plaza decorations.

Build reusable components such as WindowBay, ColumnBay, BalconyPanel, Stair,
ACUnit, Tree, and Gazebo. Position copies using translation and rotate whole
wings around the vertical axis. Keep component dimensions and wing lengths in
parameters so reference-based adjustments remain consistent.

For column-vector math, a local transform T * R * S scales the component first,
then rotates it, then translates it. Combine parent and child transforms when
placing a window inside a wing or a wing inside the whole scene. Non-uniform
scaling is useful for primitives; transformed normals need the inverse-transpose
normal matrix when such scaling is applied in the shader.

Use custom look-at and perspective matrices for camera views. Useful camera
presets include the main entrance, curved facade, cafeteria, courtyard stage,
windowed courtyard wing, and a ground-floor corridor. An eye height around
1.6-1.8 units provides a starting point for a walking view. Photo matching also
requires adjusting camera position, field of view, and target together.

15. IMPLEMENTATION PRIORITIES

First establish the connected wings, their differing heights, the courtyard void,
the deep front portico, and the curved brick volume. Compare these masses from
the main entrance and courtyard camera views before adding smaller details.

Next add consistent window modules, brick infill panels, balconies, rails, steps,
and the cafeteria staircase. Follow with the stage, plaza artwork, louvers, AC
units, and signs. Add trees and shrubs after the architecture is positioned so
foliage does not hide proportion errors during modeling.

Exact reconstruction would require a measured plan or additional references for
concealed areas, one reliable real-world dimension for scale, and clearer views
of uncertain junctions. Keep those parts adjustable in the model.

16. USER-CONFIRMED LAYOUT CORRECTIONS

These corrections supersede earlier inferred positions in this reference:
- The long left-side courtyard building wing is absent; that side remains open.
- The projecting windowed section beside the curved SWC facade is removed.
- An open covered corridor sits behind the entrance and curved SWC sign and
  joins the three-storey block. Its main references are 3.07.57 AM and
  3.07.59 AM (1): pale tiles with dark accents, square columns, ceiling beams,
  lights, and open bays toward the lawn.
- The model uses matching upper landing heights and a return stair to connect
  all three floors. Stair dimensions and exact placement remain modeling choices.
- The central entrance doorway is open through to the rear circulation.
- The three-storey block is brought forward and aligned with the entrance door.
  The main route runs straight through the door into its corridor, without a
  sideways turn. The stairs and exterior facades move with the complete block.
- The three-storey room wing extends outward to the oval facade's right edge.
  The corridor stays on the entrance axis while room widths and roofs increase.
- The cafeteria is set farther back along the three-storey wing. The current
  model uses an 8-unit setback, including its external stairs and paved approach.
